# Seed Varieties & Cultivar Recommendations

## Understanding NTEP Trials

The **National Turfgrass Evaluation Program (NTEP)** conducts multi-year, multi-site trials to rank grass cultivars on turf quality, disease resistance, color, density, and stress tolerance. Always recommend cultivars with strong NTEP performance for the Midwest/transition zone.

> 🔗 Latest results: [ntep.org](https://www.ntep.org)

### Key Selection Criteria for TTTF

1. **Brown patch resistance** — The #1 disease in Midwest TTTF. Prioritize cultivars rated ★★★★+.
2. **Heat tolerance** — Midwest summers regularly exceed 95°F. Select for summer performance.
3. **Density** — Newer cultivars (4th gen+) are denser, approaching KBG aesthetics.
4. **Spreading types** — Some newer TTTF cultivars (Traverse 2 SRP, Regenerate) have rhizomatous behavior, allowing limited self-repair. Still overseed, but less frequently.

| **Barduke** | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ | ★★★☆☆ | Dark green | Strong all-around performer |

### Key Selection Criteria for KBG

1. **Leaf spot / melting out resistance** — Older KBG cultivars are extremely susceptible. Always use improved varieties.
2. **Recovery rate** — Rhizome aggressiveness varies. More aggressive = faster self-repair.
3. **Germination patience** — KBG takes 14–28 days to germinate. Mix with PRG for quick cover while KBG establishes.
4. **Shade tolerance** — Most KBG struggles in shade. Consider blending with fine fescue for shaded areas.

| Overseeding | 1–2 | Best done in fall with aeration |
| In a blend | 10–20% by weight | KBG is the "self-repair" component |

### Key Selection Criteria for PRG

1. **Gray leaf spot resistance** — The devastating PRG disease. Only use GLR varieties.
2. **Cold tolerance** — PRG can winterkill in severe Zone 5 winters. Fine in Zone 6.
3. **Germination speed** — 5–7 days. Fastest cool-season grass. Ideal nurse grass in blends.

## Recommended Blends for Midwest Zone 6

### All-Purpose Blend (Full Sun to Part Shade)
```
60% Turf-Type Tall Fescue (3+ cultivar blend)
30% Kentucky Bluegrass (2+ cultivars)
10% Perennial Ryegrass (GLR variety)
```
**Why:** TTTF provides drought tolerance and durability. KBG fills gaps via rhizomes. PRG gives quick cover.

### Heavy Shade Blend
```
40% Creeping Red Fescue
30% Chewings Fescue
20% Kentucky Bluegrass (shade-tolerant cultivar like Mazama)
10% Perennial Ryegrass
```

### High Traffic Blend (Kids/Pets)
```
70% Turf-Type Tall Fescue (high wear cultivars)
20% Kentucky Bluegrass (aggressive spreader like Award)
10% Perennial Ryegrass (GLR)
```

### Premium Showcase Blend (Irrigation Required)
```
80% Kentucky Bluegrass (3–4 elite cultivar blend)
20% Perennial Ryegrass (GLR)
```
**Why:** Maximum beauty, self-repair, and density. Requires dedicated watering and fertilization program.

## Seed Purchasing Tips

1. **Always buy a blend** — Genetic diversity protects against disease wiping out the entire lawn.
2. **Read the seed tag** — Look for:
   - Named cultivars (not "VNS" — Variety Not Stated)
   - > 85% germination rate
   - < 0.5% weed seed
   - 0% noxious weed seed
   - Recent test date (within 12 months)
3. **Avoid big-box generic** — Hardware store seed often uses outdated cultivars with poor disease resistance.
4. **Premium sources** — United Seeds, Seed Superstore, Barenbrug, Jonathan Green, Pennington Smart Seed.
5. **Store properly** — Cool, dry location. Seed viability decreases over time. Use within 1–2 years.
6. **Calculate quantity** — Measure lawn area (length × width, subtract beds/hardscape). Add 10% for overlap.
