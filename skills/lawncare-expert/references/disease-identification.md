# Lawn Disease Identification & Treatment

## Fungal Diseases

### Brown Patch (*Rhizoctonia solani*)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Circular patches 6"–several feet, tan/brown center with dark "smoke ring" border |
| **Season** | Summer (night temps > 68°F + high humidity) |
| **Favored by** | Excessive nitrogen, evening watering, poor air flow |
| **Susceptible** | Tall fescue (primary target), perennial ryegrass |
| **Cultural control** | Reduce N in summer. Water early AM only. Improve drainage. Mow at proper height. |
| **Chemical control** | Azoxystrobin (Heritage), propiconazole (Banner Maxx), myclobutanil (Eagle). Apply preventively when conditions are right. |
| **Recovery** | Turf usually recovers in fall. Overseed bare spots in September. |

---

### Dollar Spot (*Clarireedia jacksonii*)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Silver-dollar-sized tan spots. Hourglass-shaped lesions on individual blades. Morning dew reveals white mycelium. |
| **Season** | Late spring through fall (60–85°F) |
| **Favored by** | Low nitrogen, drought stress, heavy dew |
| **Susceptible** | Kentucky bluegrass, fine fescue, perennial ryegrass |
| **Cultural control** | Apply nitrogen (the disease feeds on N-starved turf). Water deeply but infrequently. Remove morning dew with a drag hose. |
| **Chemical control** | Propiconazole, thiophanate-methyl. Rotate fungicide classes. |

---

### Pythium Blight (*Pythium* spp.)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Greasy, dark, water-soaked patches. Cottony white mycelium in early morning. Patches streak in the direction of mowing or water flow. |
| **Season** | Summer hot spells (temps > 85°F + high humidity + poor drainage) |
| **Favored by** | Overwatering, compacted soil, poor drainage |
| **Susceptible** | Perennial ryegrass (most susceptible), Kentucky bluegrass |
| **Cultural control** | Improve drainage. Avoid watering in evening. Reduce compaction with aeration. |
| **Chemical control** | Mefenoxam (Subdue Maxx), fosetyl-Al (Aliette). Must be applied BEFORE symptoms appear — once visible, damage is done. |

---

### Red Thread (*Laetisaria fuciformis*)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Pinkish-red thread-like strands extending from blade tips. Irregular patches, 4–12" diameter. |
| **Season** | Spring and fall (60–75°F + wet conditions) |
| **Favored by** | Low nitrogen, excessive moisture |
| **Susceptible** | Fine fescue, perennial ryegrass |
| **Cultural control** | Apply nitrogen — this is the primary fix. Disease rarely kills turf. Improve air circulation. |
| **Chemical control** | Usually unnecessary. Propiconazole if cosmetically unacceptable. |

---

### Rust (*Puccinia* spp.)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Orange-yellow powder on blades and shoes after mowing. Turf may appear orange from a distance. |
| **Season** | Late summer to fall (warm days, cool nights, dew) |
| **Favored by** | Slow-growing turf, drought stress, shade |
| **Susceptible** | Kentucky bluegrass, perennial ryegrass |
| **Cultural control** | Apply nitrogen to stimulate growth (outgrow the disease). Mow regularly. Water properly. |
| **Chemical control** | Rarely needed. Propiconazole or azoxystrobin if severe. |

---

### Snow Mold (Pink & Gray)

| Attribute | Pink (*Microdochium nivale*) | Gray (*Typhula* spp.) |
|-----------|-----|------|
| **Appearance** | Pink-tinged matted circles at snowmelt | Gray-white matted circles with sclerotia |
| **Season** | Late winter / early spring after snow cover | Same |
| **Favored by** | Long grass going into winter, heavy snow cover, unfrozen ground | Same + extended snow cover |
| **Cultural control** | Lower final mow height (2.5–3"). Avoid late-fall N (promotes lush growth). Clear snow piles from lawn. |
| **Chemical control** | Preventive fungicide in late fall before first snow if historically problematic. |

---

### Leaf Spot / Melting Out (*Bipolaris* / *Drechslera* spp.)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Dark brown/purple lesions on blades → progresses to "melting out" where entire crown rots |
| **Season** | Spring (leaf spot) → Summer (melting out) |
| **Favored by** | Excessive nitrogen in spring, low mowing height, wet conditions |
| **Susceptible** | Kentucky bluegrass (older cultivars especially) |
| **Cultural control** | Mow high (3"+). Avoid spring over-fertilization. Select improved KBG cultivars resistant to leaf spot. |
| **Chemical control** | Chlorothalonil (Daconil), iprodione if severe. |

---

## Insect Damage

### White Grubs (Japanese Beetle, June Bug, European Chafer larvae)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Irregular brown patches in late summer/fall. Turf peels back like carpet — roots are severed. |
| **Threshold** | > 10 grubs per sq ft requires treatment |
| **Detection** | Cut a 1 sq ft section, peel back, count C-shaped grubs |
| **Preventive** | Chlorantraniliprole (GrubEx) — apply June, before eggs hatch. Lasts all season. |
| **Curative** | Trichlorfon (Dylox) — fast-acting, use when active grubs are found. Water in immediately. |
| **Natural** | Milky spore (Paenibacillus popilliae) — targets Japanese beetle only. Takes 2–3 years to establish. Nematodes (Heterorhabditis bacteriophora) — apply in August–September to moist soil. |

---

### Chinch Bugs (*Blissus* spp.)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Irregular yellow-brown patches, usually starting along driveways/sidewalks (heat sinks). Does NOT peel back. |
| **Season** | Summer heat stress (July–August) |
| **Detection** | Flotation test: Push a coffee can into soil, fill with water. Chinch bugs float up in 10 min. |
| **Treatment** | Bifenthrin or trichlorfon. Water in after application. |

---

### Sod Webworms

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Irregular brown patches with blades chewed off at soil level. Green fecal pellets (frass) visible. Small tan moths flying over lawn at dusk. |
| **Season** | Late spring through summer |
| **Detection** | Soap flush test: 2 Tbsp dish soap in 1 gallon water, pour over 1 sq ft. Larvae come to surface in 10 min. |
| **Treatment** | Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) for organic. Bifenthrin for conventional. |

## Abiotic Stress

### Drought Stress vs. Disease

| Indicator | Drought | Disease |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Pattern** | Follows sun exposure / slopes | Irregular or circular patches |
| **Blade appearance** | Rolled/folded, blue-gray | Lesions, discoloration, matting |
| **Footprint test** | Blades stay flat after walking | Blades spring back (or are matted) |
| **Response to water** | Recovers within 24–48 hours | No improvement with watering |

### Heat Stress

- Cool-season grasses slow growth above **80°F** and enter survival mode above **90°F**
- Do NOT fertilize during heat stress — it forces growth the plant can't support
- Maintain maximum mow height (4" for TTTF)
- Consider syringing (brief, midday misting to cool canopy — NOT a replacement for deep watering)

### Compaction

| Signs | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Water pooling, hard soil surface | Core aerate in fall |
| Thin turf despite proper fertility | Aerate + topdress with compost |
| Weeds thriving (plantain, knotweed) | These are compaction indicators — aerate first, then treat weeds |
